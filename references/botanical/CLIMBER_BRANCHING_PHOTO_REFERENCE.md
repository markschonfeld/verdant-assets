# Climber branching and mature growth reference

Production reference for the combined **Solandra maxima** + **Aristolochia
macrophylla** blast-door trellis. The photos below are external references only;
no third-party image is stored in this repository.

## Botanical conclusion

The single-stem-with-decorations model is incorrect for a mature specimen.
Mature growth should be constructed as a hierarchy:

1. several retained structural leaders;
2. lateral shoots produced by axillary buds at nodes;
3. shorter tertiary shoots on retained laterals;
4. repeated parallel runs, support changes, crossings, one-bay bridges, and
   hanging ends;
5. leaves and flowers distributed across those live branch orders.

One terminology correction matters: **the leaf does not turn into a branch**.
An axillary bud lies in the angle between leaf and stem and can produce a lateral
branch or flower. The leaf and new shoot share a node. OpenStax's stem-anatomy
reference states this explicitly.

## Species distinction

### Solandra maxima

- A vigorous woody climber that can also be pruned into a shrub with overhanging
  twigs.
- Mature photographs show a visible woody lattice under dense glossy foliage,
  with many long arching shoots occupying the same volume.
- The large flowers are usually solitary, but a mature, repeatedly branched
  specimen can carry many solitary cups at once because many flowering shoots
  are active.
- Production read: heavier scaffold, more open stem visibility, broad arching
  laterals, cups distributed across numerous retained laterals.

### Aristolochia macrophylla

- A deciduous woody twiner valued specifically for large overlapping leaves and
  dense cover of walls, arbors, pillars, and trellises.
- Mature habit is a curtain made by many thin leaders and laterals, not one vine.
  The stems disappear behind layered heart-shaped leaves.
- Baseline flowers are small relative to the leaves and are commonly hidden by
  the foliage. A profuse visible pipe-flower display would therefore be a
  **VERDANT mutation/story override**, not the normal *A. macrophylla* read.
- Production read: thinner crisscrossing/twining network, heavier foliage
  occlusion, sparse partly concealed flowers.

## Procedural construction brief for Claude

Treat the diagram's foliage as deliberately opened so the topology remains
legible. The final rendered canopy should be **three to five overlapping foliage
layers deep** in mature zones.

1. **Primary scaffold:** begin with 3–5 structural leaders per jamb/head zone for
   the combined planting. Vary age, diameter, origin, and path.
2. **Axillary branching:** choose retained nodes along each mature primary and
   spawn a lateral at the leaf axil. Do not spawn one leaf and one flower directly
   from every node with no branch hierarchy.
3. **Secondary behavior:** let some laterals run close and roughly parallel to an
   older leader, while others cross to a neighboring trellis member, bridge one
   bay, or drop unsupported.
4. **Tertiary density:** generate shorter leaf- and flower-bearing shoots from
   mature laterals. Cull irregularly; avoid radial or perfectly alternating
   procedural regularity.
5. **Corner mass:** accumulate old primaries and crossing secondaries around the
   jamb-to-head turn, then hang younger tertiary growth below it.
6. **Solandra layer:** use fewer, thicker, more openly visible stems with arching
   laterals. Distribute solitary cups and buds among many terminal/axillary
   flowering shoots.
7. **Aristolochia layer:** use more thin twining leaders and laterals, then obscure
   much of that network with overlapping leaf cards. Keep baseline pipe flowers
   sparse and partly hidden.
8. **Combined silhouette:** the species should interpenetrate rather than occupy
   two clean halves. Let the Solandra cups puncture the darker Aristolochia leaf
   curtain. Young tips still reach toward warmth and the door per VERDANT story
   logic.

The counts above are production heuristics, not measured botanical constants.
