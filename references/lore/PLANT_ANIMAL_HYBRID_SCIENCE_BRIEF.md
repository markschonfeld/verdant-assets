# Plant–animal hybrid science and VERDANT lore brief

**Purpose:** Ground the antagonist and subordinate organisms in real biology without claiming that modern science has produced a whole plant–animal creature.

**Status:** Research reference and proposed canon, not a laboratory protocol.

## Executive answer

A true plant–animal hybrid organism has not been made. There is no credible route in which a plant genome and an animal genome are crossed like two animal species and then develop into one viable body. Their cell architecture, embryonic patterning, gene regulation, metabolism, and tissue organization are far too different.

The frightening part is that several pieces of the idea already exist separately:

- Mammalian cells have taken up isolated chloroplasts in culture. In 2024, chloroplasts inside Chinese hamster ovary cells retained measurable photosystem-II electron transport for at least two days, though activity declined by day four.
- Some sea slugs retain chloroplasts stolen from algae inside their own cells for months. A 2025 study found that the slugs house them in host-made compartments called **kleptosomes**, which regulate the chloroplast environment and later digest the chloroplasts as stored food.
- Green algae naturally enter tissues and cells of developing spotted-salamander embryos. This is a real vertebrate–alga intracellular association, although evidence that the algae supply meaningful photosynthetic energy to the salamander is weak.
- Corals, anemones, hydra, giant clams, and other animals maintain living photosynthetic microbes within or among their tissues.
- Tunicates are animals that manufacture cellulose. Their cellulose-synthase ability appears to descend from an ancient horizontally transferred microbial gene.
- Photosynthetic microorganisms introduced into tadpoles have produced enough oxygen under light to restore activity in oxygen-deprived neural tissue in an experimental preparation.

The most credible VERDANT explanation is therefore **not a cross-kingdom zygote**. It is an engineered **composite organism or holobiont**: an animal developmental scaffold containing photosynthetic symbionts and chloroplast-bearing cells, surrounded by cellulose-rich protective tissues and plant-like regenerative growth zones. Its components remain biologically distinct but are metabolically and developmentally locked together.

The one-sentence horror hook is:

> Animals already steal chloroplasts, a vertebrate embryo already admits algae into its cells, and one animal lineage already makes cellulose. Eden Prime did not invent those facts; it forced them to coexist.

## 1. What has actually been achieved

### 1.1 Chloroplasts functioning inside mammalian cells

Aoki and colleagues isolated chloroplasts from the extremophilic red alga *Cyanidioschyzon merolae* and co-cultured them with Chinese hamster ovary (CHO-K1) cells. A minority of the animal cells incorporated chloroplasts. Electron microscopy showed chloroplasts in intracellular vesicles, often near the nucleus and surrounded by mitochondria.

The important result was narrow but real: incorporated chloroplasts retained photosystem-II electron-transport activity for **at least two days**. By day four, activity had declined significantly and chloroplast structure showed deterioration. The experiment did not create a photosynthetic hamster, a self-sustaining animal cell lineage, or an organism able to inherit chloroplasts.

This study has a useful historical precursor. In 1969, Margaret Nass reported uptake of isolated plant chloroplasts by cultured mouse fibroblasts. The organelles could enter animal cells, but the study did not establish durable photosynthetic function. VERDANT can therefore place crude “green animal cell” experiments within its atomic-age research program without importing modern CRISPR-era technology.

**Lore value:** The Institute's 1960s result should be the cruder one: animal
cells visibly take up green organelles, without proof that they photosynthesize
or persist. A repeatable forty-eight-hour light response must come later in the
program or be acknowledged as an alternate-history acceleration far beyond the
period's public science. The cover-up begins with an anomalous dish, not a
monster.

### 1.2 Kleptoplasty: animals that steal chloroplasts

Sacoglossan sea slugs feed on algae and retain chloroplasts in cells lining their digestive system. The chloroplasts remain structurally intact and photosynthetically active for periods ranging from days to months, depending on species. They are acquired during feeding rather than inherited as normal animal organelles.

A 2025 *Cell* study identified a host-derived organelle—the **kleptosome**—that surrounds stolen chloroplasts in sea slugs. ATP-sensitive ion channels help maintain a suitable internal environment. During starvation, the host can digest the retained chloroplasts, making the system both a photosynthetic association and a “living larder.”

This matters more to VERDANT than a simple gene splice. It demonstrates that an animal cell can evolve machinery that decides not to digest a foreign chloroplast immediately, maintains it in a specialized compartment, benefits from its chemistry, and later recycles it.

It does **not** prove that chloroplasts can power a large, active animal. Nor does it prove that algal genes have broadly moved into the slug genome. An influential early report of algal-to-slug horizontal gene transfer was not supported by later analysis of slug egg DNA. Current explanations emphasize chloroplast robustness, host cellular control, photoprotection, and specialized compartments rather than a wholesale transfer of the algal genome.

**Lore value:** The crucial fictional organ is not a leaf. It is a host-made containment vesicle that keeps foreign photosynthetic machinery alive.

### 1.3 Whole photosynthetic cells living with animals

Keeping an intact alga is easier than keeping only its chloroplast because the alga retains its nucleus and the genes required to maintain and repair photosynthesis.

Real examples include:

- Corals and anemones hosting photosynthetic dinoflagellates.
- Green hydra hosting *Chlorella* inside animal cells in a metabolically codependent relationship.
- Giant clams and other marine animals arranging photosymbionts in light-exposed tissues.
- The spotted salamander *Ambystoma maculatum*, whose embryos share an association with the green alga *Oophila amblystomatis*. Algal cells enter embryonic tissues and individual host cells.

The salamander example is especially valuable because it is a vertebrate and the association begins during development, before a mature adaptive immune system would be expected to reject foreign cells aggressively. However, later work found no convincing bulk evidence that photosynthetically fixed carbon supplies significant energy to the salamander embryo. The symbiosis is real; the “solar-powered salamander” interpretation is overstated.

**Lore value:** Institute personnel learn that the timing of exposure matters. Adult tissues reject or digest the inoculum; embryos accept it as part of the developmental environment.

### 1.4 An animal can make a “plant” structural material

Tunicates are chordate animals that synthesize cellulose and incorporate it into an external tunic. A functional cellulose-synthase gene was characterized in *Ciona* and resembles microbial cellulose synthases. This is a strong example of a conspicuously plant-associated material becoming part of an animal body plan through evolutionary gene transfer and developmental integration.

Cellulose alone does not produce wood, bark, roots, or leaves. Wood also depends on specialized plant tissues, lignified cell walls, vascular growth, and a plant developmental program. But cellulose proves that “animal tissue cannot make plant material” is too absolute.

**Lore value:** The creatures' bark-like armor need not be literal bark. It can be a cellulose–protein composite secreted by an animal epidermis, mineralized and stained with phenolic compounds. Close inspection should reveal that it is anatomically wrong for both wood and skin.

### 1.5 Photosynthesis can support tissue locally without powering the whole animal

In a 2021 experiment, microalgae and cyanobacteria distributed through the vasculature of *Xenopus* tadpoles produced oxygen under light and restored activity in oxygen-deprived neural tissue. This was an acute experimental preparation, not a stable photosynthetic vertebrate, but it shows that photosynthetic partners can alter local animal physiology.

The useful distinction is between:

- **local oxygenation or metabolite support**, which is experimentally plausible; and
- **supplying the total energy budget of a large moving animal**, which is not.

A human-sized organism has too little light-exposed surface area and too much metabolic demand to live by ordinary photosynthesis alone. Active locomotion, rapid healing, and predation are expensive. VERDANT's organisms should still require organic food, stored carbohydrate, and mineral nutrients.

**Lore value:** Light keeps peripheral tissues viable, slows starvation, and supports dormancy. It does not eliminate hunger.

## 2. Why a literal plant–animal hybrid fails

### Different developmental operating systems

An animal embryo establishes axes, germ layers, organs, muscles, nerves, and a mobile body through animal signaling networks. A plant embryo establishes meristems, roots, shoots, vascular tissues, and modular growth through different networks. Combining chromosomes would not cause the two programs to negotiate a sensible intermediate. It would cause regulatory incompatibility and developmental failure.

### Cell walls prevent ordinary tissue behavior

Most plant cells are enclosed by rigid walls. Animal development depends heavily on cell migration, membrane remodeling, flexible extracellular matrix, and the ability of cells to change neighbors. A cell cannot simultaneously behave like a rigid walled plant cell and a migrating animal embryonic cell without compartmentalizing those roles.

### Chloroplasts are not autonomous solar batteries

During endosymbiotic evolution, most genes needed by chloroplasts moved to the host nucleus or were lost. Thousands of chloroplast proteins are now nuclear-encoded, made in the cytosol, and imported through specialized TOC/TIC membrane machinery. A chloroplast placed in an ordinary animal cell lacks this maintenance network. That is why short-lived activity is much easier than stable inheritance.

### Immune surveillance and intracellular digestion

Animal cells normally route engulfed material toward lysosomal destruction, while vertebrate immune systems detect foreign cells and molecules. Successful photosymbioses require the host to tolerate, contain, feed, and regulate the symbiont without letting it overgrow.

### Energy and geometry

Photosynthesis is surface-limited. Animal muscle and nervous tissue are volume-demanding and energy-intensive. A creature cannot run, fight, and think on the light falling on a few square metres of green skin. Photosynthetic tissue can supplement metabolism, oxygenate poorly perfused regions, and extend dormancy; it cannot plausibly replace feeding.

### Radiation does not design useful monsters

Ionizing radiation produces random mutations and large amounts of damage. Mutation breeding works by generating variation and screening enormous populations for rare useful traits. Radiation should be the Institute's **variation engine and selection pressure**, not a magical instruction that tells flesh to grow leaves.

## 3. Recommended VERDANT canon: the Cultivation Composite

### 3.1 What the organism really is

Institute documentation should call the result a **composite**, **association**, or **integrated culture**, not a hybrid. “Plant–animal hybrid” is the survivor's description.

The organism has four cooperating layers:

1. **Animal chassis** — supplies embryonic body plan, nervous system, muscle, circulation, sensory organs, and behavior.
2. **Intracellular photobiont** — a selected alga or cyanobacterium inhabits light-exposed animal cells inside host-made symbiosomes.
3. **Kleptoplast tissue** — some digestive and regenerative cells retain chloroplasts from the photobiont inside kleptosome-like compartments for short-term oxygen and carbon chemistry.
4. **Cellulose-rich dermal growth** — modified epidermal cells secrete a cellulose–protein matrix that forms plates, fibers, tendrils, and false bark without becoming genuine plant wood.

The visible “plant” is therefore a combination of green symbiotic cells, animal-grown structural polymer, and branching regenerative tissue. There are no ordinary plant cells distributed through the muscles or brain.

### 3.2 The single major fictional leap

The Institute succeeds in making the association **developmentally self-renewing**.

Real animals can host algae; real animal cells can temporarily host chloroplasts; real animals can synthesize cellulose. Modern science cannot make all three systems develop together, reproduce together, and remain stable across a large vertebrate body.

VERDANT's fictional breakthrough is a host-derived compartment called in
internal notes the **green body**. It combines the containment function of a
symbiosome with the environmental control and timed digestion seen in a
kleptosome. Host proteins regulate the compartment's ion balance, acidity,
retention, and recycling; they do **not** replace the thousands of
nuclear-encoded photobiont proteins needed to maintain a chloroplast indefinitely.

The intact photobiont remains the self-maintaining photosynthetic partner. It
divides inside protected nursery tissues and is partitioned into daughter cells
or reseeds new tissue during growth. Loose chloroplasts are temporary cargo:
specialized animal cells acquire them from the photobiont, use them for a
limited period, digest damaged ones, and replenish them. The organism inherits
the machinery and tolerance for that cycle rather than an immortal chloroplast.

Do not claim that the Institute transferred an entire plant genome. Its
achievement is control of **cell trafficking, immune tolerance, developmental
inheritance, and organelle replenishment**.

### 3.3 How an atomic-age program reaches it

The period-plausible route is empirical selection, not precision editing:

1. **Mutation-breeding program:** crop, algal, fungal, and animal cell cultures are exposed to graded radiation and chemical mutagens. Most die or become useless. The Institute keeps rare lines with unusual organelle retention, stress tolerance, pigmentation, or extracellular fibers.
2. **Cell-culture screening:** researchers test which animal cells engulf algae or chloroplasts without immediately digesting them. Green persistence becomes the first crude success metric.
3. **Embryonic passage:** surviving associations are introduced progressively earlier in development. Embryonic tolerance allows foreign cells to become part of the organism's expected biological environment.
4. **Serial selection:** each generation or tissue passage is selected for longer retention, lower inflammation, better partitioning during cell division, and survival under light/dark cycles.
5. **Regenerative fixation:** selected tissues are coupled to wound-healing and stem-cell niches. Regrowth reproduces the composite architecture more reliably than sexual reproduction.
6. **Closed-system evolution:** after Eden Prime is sealed, decades of escape, grafting, predation, and selection finish what the researchers began. The present organisms are not identical to the Institute specimens.

This route lets the 1961 facility begin with techniques consistent with its era while allowing the mature horror to emerge over roughly sixty years. It also preserves the project's existing gamma-garden logic: radiation creates a broad field of damage and variation; selection, husbandry, and containment failure create the lineage.

### 3.4 Why the antagonist differs from the minions

The antagonist should be the only deeply integrated, neurologically continuous composite. The subordinate organisms can be **ramets**—clonal offshoots grown from regenerative nodules, grafted animal tissue, and symbiont-rich nursery beds.

That distinction explains why:

- the antagonist retains memory, planning, and identity;
- minions show simplified or specialized behavior;
- minions share chemical recognition and do not attack the parent;
- bodies can vary according to the animal tissue, nursery substrate, light, and nutrients used during growth;
- a damaged minion cannot simply regenerate into another antagonist.

A literal telepathic hive mind is unnecessary. Coordination can use animal sensory cues, volatile compounds, vibration, pheromones, and conditioned responses. If a networked intelligence is desired, treat it as a further fictional layer rather than a known plant ability.

## 4. Physiology that will make the creatures feel real

### Light is maintenance, not food replacement

- In bright conditions, exposed green tissues make oxygen and small carbon contributions.
- During immobility, this slows depletion and supports repair.
- During pursuit or combat, the animal metabolism dominates and rapidly consumes stored reserves.
- After exertion, a creature may root, spread light-exposed flaps, or press against warm illuminated surfaces.
- Prolonged darkness does not kill it immediately; it increases hunger, tissue loss, and predatory behavior.

### Rooting is feeding and repair

“Roots” should be composite feeding organs rather than true botanical roots. They invade wet substrate, carcasses, plumbing biofilms, or soil and absorb water, ions, microbial metabolites, and digested organics. A rooted organism can shunt resources into cellulose deposition and wound closure.

### Green tissue belongs near surfaces

Photosynthetic tissue should cluster in thin, vascularized, light-exposed areas: translucent skin, fronds, dorsal folds, facial growths, or subdermal chambers. Thick green muscle deep in the body would receive too little light and should read as diseased or vestigial rather than productive.

### False bark should crack like a living composite

The armor is secreted in layers. New material remains flexible and wet near the body; old material dries, mineralizes, and splits. Under a broken plate should be vascular animal tissue, not concentric tree rings. The surface can carry lichens, algae, or opportunistic plants without those organisms being part of the core body.

### Photosynthesis creates liabilities

Light-driven chemistry produces reactive oxygen species when the system is stressed or oversaturated. Real photosynthetic organisms require photoprotection and repair. This supports:

- mottled bleaching around wounds;
- dark pigments over overloaded green tissue;
- avoidance of sudden intense light despite attraction to usable diffuse light;
- heat and oxidative injury in sealed transparent spaces;
- periodic shedding or digestion of damaged plastid bodies.

Bright light should not function as a simple vampire weakness. The frighteningly credible behavior is regulation: the creatures seek useful light but shield injured tissue from too much of it.

### Reproduction is vegetative and unreliable

The composites should not breed as a stable new species. They propagate through managed or accidental budding, graft fragments, eggs inoculated with symbionts, or growths cultured in nutrient beds. Many attempts fail. The malformed bodies in Eden Prime are the visible waste stream of a system that only occasionally produces a viable ramet.

## 5. Suggested discovery ladder for the player

The explanation will be more effective if the player encounters real facts before the final fictional leap.

1. **Early laboratory note:** animal cells remained green for forty-eight hours after chloroplast uptake.
2. **Comparative-biology card:** sea slugs retain stolen chloroplasts; salamander embryos contain algae.
3. **Materials report:** cellulose detected in an animal-derived epidermal culture, compared with tunicates.
4. **Failure report:** irradiated specimens mostly died, became sterile, or formed tumors; useful traits required serial selection.
5. **Containment report:** adult inoculation failed, while embryonic exposure produced immune tolerance.
6. **Pathology report:** photosynthetic tissue supplies local oxygen but does not meet whole-body energy demand; specimens consume protein aggressively after exertion or darkness.
7. **Final revelation:** the antagonist is not half plant and half animal. It is an animal intelligence that turned multiple organisms and organelles into inheritable body parts.

A concise in-world line:

> “We never crossed a plant with an animal. We taught the animal which parts not to digest.”

## 6. Claims to avoid

Avoid these explanations because they weaken the science-horror effect:

- **“Radiation fused plant and animal DNA.”** Radiation is random damage, not an intelligent splicing tool.
- **“Chlorophyll lets them live entirely on sunlight.”** Their energy budget is far too large.
- **“Plant cells became neurons.”** Preserve animal nervous tissue.
- **“A single plant gene produced leaves, roots, and wood.”** Complex morphology requires developmental systems, not one trait gene.
- **“Sea slugs inherited the whole algal photosynthesis pathway.”** Broad algal-to-slug germline gene transfer is not supported by later genomic work.
- **“The salamander is naturally solar-powered.”** Intracellular algae are real; substantial photosynthate transfer remains unproven.
- **“The organism is a fertile new species.”** A clonally maintained composite is much more credible.

## Bottom line

VERDANT does not need to pretend that science can cross a person with a plant. The more credible and disturbing premise is that the Institute assembled a developmental alliance from mechanisms evolution has already discovered separately: intracellular symbiosis, organelle theft, embryonic tolerance, horizontal acquisition of metabolic traits, and relentless selection.

The result is frightening because it is not one impossible miracle. It is several real biological exceptions made to cooperate—and then left to cultivate themselves for sixty years.
